LAB 6 MANUAL EVIDENCE

1. Tokenization
Input: I can't log in.
Whitespace tokens: ["I", "can't", "log", "in."] -> 4
Word-like regex tokens: ["I", "can", "t", "log", "in"] -> 5
Reason: whitespace keeps punctuation attached to tokens, while the word-like regex extracts word characters and splits the contraction.

Input: help@example.com
The regex breaks the email into smaller word-like pieces instead of preserving the complete address as one token.

2. Chunking
Fixed window size = 40 words.
Overlap 0 uses non-overlapping windows.
Overlap 10 reuses 10 words between neighboring windows, which increases repeated content.
Sentence-aware chunking tries to keep sentence boundaries but still splits a sentence longer than 40 words.

3. Retrieval
Hit@3 = 1 when at least one relevant chunk is in the first three results; otherwise 0.
MRR@3 = reciprocal of the rank of the first relevant result, or 0 for a miss.
The notebook evaluates 8 answerable and 2 unanswerable queries.

4. Responsible evidence
A high similarity score is not proof that the retrieved text is correct. An unanswerable query may still receive a plausible-looking result, so the assistant exposes the evidence and source instead of silently presenting it as a verified answer.
